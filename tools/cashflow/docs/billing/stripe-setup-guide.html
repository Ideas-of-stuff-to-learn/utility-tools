Stripe Setup

Getting your Stripe account ready

Follow these steps in order. You only need to do this once.

About 10–15 minutes
Before you start

Why you need to set this up yourself — and why it only takes 15 minutes

Your developer has built the entire payment system: the code that processes cards, manages subscriptions, sends receipts, and handles billing errors. That part is done. The one thing a developer cannot do on your behalf is create your Stripe account — because Stripe legally requires that the person or business receiving the money is the one who owns the account. Your identity, your bank account, and your tax information must be verified directly by Stripe. This document was written to guide you through that short process step by step.

🃏
What your developer will use to test — fake card numbers. In test mode, Stripe provides a list of made-up card numbers that simulate real payments without charging anyone. Your developer will use these to check that the checkout flow works before any real customers pay. The most common one is 4242 4242 4242 4242 — it always succeeds. There are others for testing a declined card, a wrong CVV, etc.

Where to find them: In the Stripe dashboard, go to Developers → Testing — or search "Stripe test cards" in your browser. They are publicly listed by Stripe and you do not need to send them to your developer; your developer already knows about them. You do not need to do anything with these — this is purely for your awareness.
🔴
When you eventually switch to Live mode — what changes
💳 Real money moves. Every card charge is processed against a real bank card. The fake test card numbers Stripe provides (like 4242 4242 4242 4242) that your developer used during testing will no longer be accepted — from this point only real cards from real customers go through.
🔑 You get a separate set of live keys. The API keys you're collecting today start with pk_test_ and sk_test_. Live keys start with pk_live_ and sk_live_. They are completely separate and both equally sensitive.
🏦 Your bank account must be connected first. Stripe will not let you go live until your identity is verified and your payout bank account is on file. (The bank account steps are at the bottom of this guide

B2
Verify your identity with Stripe
Before Stripe will let money flow to your bank, they need to confirm who you are. This is a legal requirement they handle directly — your developer is not involved in this step.
  • 1Stripe will prompt you to verify your identity — click Start verification
  • 2Have your passport or driving licence ready — Stripe scans it through their own secure system
  • 3You may also need to provide your date of birth and home address
  • 4This is handled entirely by Stripe — none of this information is seen by your developer
💡
Why does Stripe need this? Financial regulations (specifically anti-money-laundering laws) require that any business accepting card payments verifies who the money is going to. Stripe carries out these checks on behalf of the card networks. It is entirely normal and every business that accepts cards goes through it.
B3
Add your bank account details
This is the account where Stripe will send your money. It must be a UK bank account if you're a UK business.
  • 1Click Add bank account
  • 2Enter your sort code (6 digits, format XX-XX-XX)
  • 3Enter your account number (8 digits)
  • 4Enter the name on the account — must match your Stripe account name
  • 5Click Save — Stripe may send a small verification deposit (under £1) to confirm the account is yours

🔐  Final reminder — key security
!
The golden rule — never send keys in a message
This is worth repeating one final time, because the consequences of getting it wrong are serious and permanent.
What is at risk if your Secret key or Webhook secret is intercepted
🚨
One final time, because it matters more than anything else in this guide. When you are ready to hand your keys to your developer — whether today's test keys or future live keys — do not message them. Not WhatsApp. Not iMessage. Not email. Not Slack. Not a photo of your screen.

The only method: schedule a video or phone call, share your screen, and watch your developer enter each key directly. You stay in control of the keys at all times. Your developer types them in on their end while you watch. The keys never exist in any message thread.

If your developer asks you to send them in a message, decline. A good developer will understand and expect a screen-share instead.
This guide is for test mode only — no real money moves until you switch to Live mode.
